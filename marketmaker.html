◆MARKET MAKER · PCP OPTIONS

Put-call-parity options market maker on Deribit.

Rest passive limit quotes on BTC options priced off put-call parity against the dated future (C = P + (F − K)/F). Quote at parity ± margin so that, if filled, the completing hedge (opposite option + future) locks a small positive PnL — payment for providing liquidity. A quote is posted only when the edge survives modeled hedge slippage, fees and a safety buffer.

◈ Inside the simulator — the stack on real captured data

Alpha · Hard-capped burn budget Real fills landed for the first time on 2026-09-09. Three sections below show real activity on Deribit production: a single-order latency test and a full bulk mass-quoting smoke test, both zero-fill by design (post-only, far from the market, minimum size) — and Phase D, where a full put-call-parity package (put, call, future hedge) was actually opened and closed for real, a small bounded loss against a hard-capped ~0.0102 BTC burn budget, not the strategy resting live. Validation still follows a live-first ladder (six phases, A–F, replacing the original testnet gate — Deribit testnet only shows other testnet users, not a realistic live picture): prove mechanics live, in bounded and monitored steps, before any capital is committed at scale. A written go/no-go is still the last step, not an early one.

▦PHASE C · BULK LIVE MARKET-MAKING TESTMass-quoting, editing, MMP and kill-switch machinery — proven live on Deribit production25SEP26 expiry · 2026-09-08
Real · Production The bigger of the two real tests on this page. Five days after the single-order latency test above, the same account ran the full bulk market-making stack live: mass-quote batching through a smart change queue with rate limiting, Market Maker Protection (MMP), proximity-based auto-cancel, and a hard kill-switch — everything phase C exists to prove, short of an actual fill. Getting a clean run took roughly twenty rounds of live attempts and fixes: a few were real protocol details (MMP field mapping, price-grid rounding, REST auth format); most were the test harness being too strict about legitimate exchange behavior — a proximity auto-cancel it didn't expect, a market drifting out of range mid-window, one truth-sweep reply that legitimately took 3 seconds against a 2-second deadline. Zero fills, zero capital ever at risk.
