DATA —

How the paper-trading simulator works.

Every chart on this page is drawn from a real recorded Deribit session — the full 28AUG26 BTC option chain (99 instruments, full depth, every update) — replayed tick-by-tick through the actual strategy stack: order books are rebuilt from the recorded feed, put-call parity (C = P + (F−K)/F) prices every strike off the dated future, the honest edge gate charges depth-walked hedge slippage plus every fee, and only quotes that still clear a minimum edge are rested in the (paper) book. Nothing here is mocked.

◫THE PIPELINEOne batch of recorded book events at a time, strictly in this order
Replay feed
feed/replay.py
Recorded Parquet re-emitted through the same interface as the live FIX feed — downstream code cannot tell disk from socket.
Order books
feed/book.py
Full-depth books rebuilt per instrument. Any inconsistency raises — a desynced sim would be a lie.
Parity fair
pricing/parity.py
Fair value of each option from its opposite leg + the dated future, at the prices the hedge could actually execute.
Honest edge gate
costs/gate.py
Depth-walked hedge repricing + maker/taker/future fees + settlement + safety buffer. Post only if net edge > minimum.
Quoter → broker
strategy/ · exec/
Passive post-only quotes rested per strike and side; cancelled the moment their hedge disappears.
Fill → hedge → blotter
sim/
A strict price cross fills a quote; the completing hedge is priced immediately at the books as they are now, and the trade is blotted with full cost accounting.
∿THE ATM STRIKE, TICK BY TICK—
Our ask (red) rests above parity fair (purple) by the full cost stack + minimum edge; our bid (green) rests below it by the same amount. The gray band is the market's own bid–ask spread. Fair value moves with the future and the put book — our quotes follow it, requoting only when the move exceeds the requote threshold.
✕HOW A FILL IS SIMULATEDThe replayed market never saw our orders — so what counts as a fill?

We can't know our place in the queue at a price level, and the recorded feed contains no trade prints — only book state. So the simulator uses the most pessimistic defensible rule: a resting quote fills only when the opposite side of the market moves strictly through its price — proof someone was willing to trade at a price worse than ours, meaning they would certainly have hit us first.

TOUCH — NOT A FILL
The ask drops to exactly our bid price. Real outcome depends on queue position we cannot know — assuming a fill here is the optimism that sank prior attempts.

STRICT CROSS — FILL
The ask moves below our bid: a seller accepted less than we were paying. They would have traded with us at our price. Filled, then hedged immediately.
0.0565market ask ↓ crossed through
0.0575our resting bid — FILLED @ 0.0575
0.0570market bid
Deliberately conservative, with known limits: touches never fill (real MMs earn some at-touch fills), fills are all-or-nothing at full quote size, and our orders leave no footprint in the replayed market. Price-wise this under-counts fills rather than over-counting them — the direction you want when the simulator is the thing deciding whether the edge is real. The testnet stage (gate 3) replaces this model with real fills from a real matching engine.
